🖊❀ AI for Literature🖊❀ AI for Literature

当文字的生成变得廉价，是什么让你依然选择拿起笔？
你执笔的初心是什么？

When the generation of words becomes cheap, what makes you still choose to pick up the pen?
What was the original intention behind your pen?

对于大部分00后来说，学生时代大概率都听过一句话：“21世纪是生物的世纪”。这句话的流行，与人类基因组计划的推进密切相关。在那个世纪之交，尽管互联网泡沫刚刚破裂，但谁也没预料到，信息技术会以指数级的姿态重塑整个世界。那是一个充满怀旧底色的年代。

For most of Gen Z, school days likely included a familiar refrain: "The 21st century is the century of biology." Its popularity was tied to the advance of the Human Genome Project. At that turn of the century, even as the dot-com bubble had just burst, no one foresaw how information technology would reshape the entire world at an exponential pace. It was an era steeped in nostalgia.

正如《New Boy》里唱的那样，从Windows 98的开机音效到Windows 10，个人电脑完成了从极客玩具到家庭基础设施的蜕变；2007年苹果手机的问世，又宣告了掌上智能时代的全面降临。而技术的下沉，带来了一场真正的文化大爆发——游戏、动漫、影视同步井喷， 从4399到Steam，从数码宝贝到鬼灭之刃，虚拟世界不再是现实的附属品，它本身就是整整一代人的第二人生。

As "New Boy" sings, from the Windows 98 startup sound to Windows 10, the personal computer evolved from a geek's toy into household infrastructure; the 2007 iPhone then heralded the full arrival of the handheld smart era. This technology cascade ignited a true cultural explosion — games, anime, and film surged at once, from 4399 to Steam, from Digimon to Demon Slayer. The virtual world was no longer an accessory to reality; it became an entire generation's second life.

那时候电视里还会插播讲孝顺、讲礼让的公益广告，动画片里藏着勇气、友情的道理，连片尾曲都带着点温柔的哲思。后来不知道从什么时候开始，公益广告从黄金时段渐渐消失，动画片也少了曾经的教导意义，多了快闪的画面、无厘头的打闹，连曾经承载一代人回忆的经典IP，都被批量魔改成毫无逻辑的流量产物。2016年抖音上线，短视频的浪潮席卷而来，15秒的刺激取代了长叙事，算法推荐的爽感盖过了需要沉淀的内容，那种所有人围着一台电视、共同被一段内容打动的集体记忆，渐渐成了时代的眼泪。

Back then, TV still aired public-service ads about filial piety and courtesy; cartoons carried quiet lessons of courage and friendship, and even their ending themes held a gentle philosophy. Yet at some point, those ads faded from prime time, cartoons lost their didactic warmth and gave way to flashy cuts and slapstick, and the classic IPs that once carried a generation's memories were mass-remade into logic-free traffic bait. When Douyin launched in 2016, short video swept everything: 15 seconds of stimulation replaced long-form narrative, algorithmic dopamine drowned out content that demanded patience, and that collective memory of a family gathered around one television, moved by one show, quietly became a relic of the age.

让我们把视线转移到文学领域。过去，文学创作是一道有门槛的手艺活。你要发表，得经过编辑筛选、出版社把关、评论家审视，一个普通人脑海中的故事，大概率会随他一起消失在时间里。传统文学的殿堂庄严而封闭，读者与作者之间隔着一道看不见的高墙。

Now turn to literature. Writing was once a craft with thresholds. To be published, your work had to pass through editors, publishing houses, and critics; the story in an ordinary person's mind would most likely vanish with them in time. The temple of traditional literature stood solemn and sealed, an invisible wall between reader and author.

网络文学的崛起，第一次凿穿了这道墙。阅文、起点确立了付费订阅与IP孵化模式，改写了与出版社的关系。玄幻、仙侠、都市、科幻百花齐放。日更、追更、打赏、催更，读者和作者之间形成了前所未有的共生关系。当然后续也带来了一些弊端。不过文学不再是少数人的特权，它变成了一场全民参与的叙事狂欢。

The rise of web fiction first broke through that wall. China Literature and Qidian established paid subscriptions and IP incubation, rewriting the relationship with publishers. Fantasy, xianxia, urban, sci-fi — all bloomed. Daily updates, binge-reading, tipping, nagging for the next chapter: an unprecedented symbiosis formed between reader and writer (with downsides to come). Literature was no longer a privilege of the few — it became a narrative carnival for everyone.

而后，以番茄小说为代表的免费阅读平台，依托算法推荐进一步改写了文学的分发逻辑。作品不再依赖编辑的人工筛选，而是由算法根据用户偏好精准推送。创作的门槛被再次压低——大量初中，乃至小学生都开始创作。

Then free-reading platforms like Fanqie (Tomato) Novel rewrote the logic of literary distribution through algorithmic recommendation. Works no longer relied on human editorial selection but on precise, preference-driven pushes. The barrier to creation dropped again — hordes of middle-schoolers, even elementary students, began to write.

从传统文学到网文，再到算法分发，每一步都在消解旧有的门槛。随之到来的疫情加速了一切。 人们的生活方式被深度数字化，紧接着，人工智能以近乎狂飙的姿态降临。从最初连0.9和0.11的大小都难以分辨，到如今拥有1M级上下文窗口；从简单对话，到AI短剧、AI影视的批量生成，甚至AI for Math开始协助证伪复杂的数学猜想。从2023年开始，人们似乎走向了一条技术狂飙的“不归路”。

From traditional literature to web fiction to algorithmic distribution, every step dissolved an old threshold. Then the pandemic accelerated everything: life went fully digital, and artificial intelligence arrived at a breakneck pace. From models that once could not tell 0.9 from 0.11, to today's 1M-token context windows; from simple chat to the mass generation of AI short dramas and films, even AI for Math now helps refute complex conjectures. Since 2023, we seem to have set foot on a "one-way road" of runaway technology.

于是，“AI for Literature”成为了一个无法回避的命题。

And so "AI for Literature" became a question we can no longer evade.

千百年来，人类对文学的定义从未停止过争论——亚里士多德说文学是对行动的摹仿，浪漫主义者说文学是情感的自然流露， 后现代主义者干脆说文学不过是权力的话语工具 。每一种定义都试图框住文学，却每次都从指缝间溜走。

For millennia, humanity has never ceased debating the definition of literature — Aristotle called it an imitation of action, the Romantics called it the natural overflow of emotion, and the postmodernists dismissed it as merely a discursive tool of power. Every definition tries to cage literature, and every time it slips through the fingers.

有人质疑文学的“欺骗性”，也有人提出一种冷峻的论断：“科技负责直线上升，而政治和文学负责螺旋。”这让人不得不追问一个更古老的问题：先有语言，还是先有文字？语言的诞生，是人类第一次将混沌的感受编码为可传递的信号；文字的诞生，则是将稍纵即逝的声音凝固为可跨越时空的印记。而大模型对语言文字的处理，某种意义上是一次“二次精炼”——它吞吐了人类数千年积累的文字，试图从中蒸馏出语言的本质。而语言又是人类对现实物理世界的二次精炼。这究竟是回归，还是另一种远离？

Some question literature's "deceptiveness"; others offer a bleak aphorism: "Technology ascends in straight lines, while politics and literature move in spirals." This forces an older question: which came first, language or writing? Language was humanity's first encoding of chaotic feeling into transmittable signals; writing froze fleeting sound into marks that cross time and space. And the large model's processing of language is, in a sense, a "second refinement" — it digests millennia of accumulated human text, trying to distill the essence of language itself, which was already our second refinement of physical reality. Is this a return, or another kind of departure?

为什么在拥有了电影、游戏等极致视听体验的今天，我们依然需要文字？因为文字拥有超越物理画面的“内在画面感”。影视的画面是具象的、被导演框定的，而文字则是留白的。一千个读者心中有一千个哈姆雷特，但一千个观众眼中只有一个银幕上的哈姆雷特。文字的力量恰恰在于它的不完整性——它邀请读者共同完成创作。

Why, in an age of cinema and games with their ultimate audiovisual experiences, do we still need words? Because words possess an "inner imagery" beyond any physical frame. A film's image is concrete, dictated by its director; text is blank space. A thousand readers hold a thousand Hamlets, but a thousand viewers see only the one on screen. The power of literature lies precisely in its incompleteness — it invites the reader to finish the creation.

而从“人人创作”的角度看，AI的到来并非终结，而是又一次门槛的消解。如果说网文平台让“会打字的人都能写”，番茄让“写出来的人都能被看见”，那么AI让“会说话的人都能写”。创作的主体在扩大，但问题也随之而来——当生成的门槛趋近于零，什么才是真正值得被阅读的文字？

From the perspective of "everyone creates," AI's arrival is not an ending but another dissolving of thresholds. If web-fiction platforms let "anyone who can type write," and Fanqie let "anyone who writes be seen," then AI lets "anyone who speaks write." The circle of creators widens — but a question follows: when the barrier to generation approaches zero, what text is truly worth reading?

AI可以一秒钟生成一万字的华丽辞藻，可以模仿任何作家的文风，但它无法生成“经历”。它可以写出“丧子之痛”，但它从未失去过任何人。

AI can generate ten thousand words of gorgeous prose in a second and mimic any writer's style — but it cannot generate "experience." It can write "the grief of losing a child," yet it has never lost anyone.

当然，谁也不明白最终的答案。如果你知道莫拉维克悖论——困难的问题是易解的，简单的问题是难解的。那么两者结合，生物智能、机械飞升是否就能通向某种完整的智能？未来会不会成为赛博朋克？现今的AI本质上仍建立在概率学之上——但谁又能保证，人脑不是另一种更精妙的概率机器呢？

Of course, no one knows the final answer. Consider the Moravec paradox — the hard problems are easy, the easy ones hard. Combined, could biological and mechanical intelligence converge into some complete intelligence? Will the future turn cyberpunk? Today's AI is still built on probability — yet who can guarantee that the human brain is not merely a more exquisite probability machine?

AI for Literature的真正意义，或许不在于让机器取代人类去创作，而在于它像一面镜子，逼迫我们重新审视文学的本质。当文字的生成变得廉价，那些真正属于人类的、带着体温与痛感的生命体验，反而会在算法的洪流中，显露出无可替代的珍贵。

The true meaning of AI for Literature may not lie in machines replacing human creation, but in its role as a mirror that forces us to re-examine the essence of literature. When the generation of words becomes cheap, those truly human experiences — warm, painful, alive — shine all the more irreplaceably amid the algorithmic flood.

AI能写出完美的结构，但写不出心跳的漏拍。 AI writes perfect structures, but misses the skipped heartbeat. 算法懂得所有词汇，却不懂眼泪的重量。 Algorithms know every word, yet not the weight of tears.
